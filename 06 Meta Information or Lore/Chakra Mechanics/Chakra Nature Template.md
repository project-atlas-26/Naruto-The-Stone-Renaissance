### Chakra Nature Template Plain Definitions
1. **Definition**  
    The fundamental meaning or concept behind the Nature. It establishes what the Nature _is intended to represent_ before specifying what it can do.^cn-def-desc
    
2. **Mechanism**  
    The underlying process by which neutral chakra becomes that Nature. It answers: **“What actually happens to chakra to make it Fire rather than Water?”**^cn-transformation-mechanism-def
    
3. **Properties**  
    The inherent characteristics the Nature possesses once transformed. Examples include temperature, density, conductivity, hardness, pressure, or electrical charge.^cn-props-def
    
4. **Behaviors**  
    How the Nature acts or responds once manifested. This includes things like spreading, flowing, accelerating, dispersing, igniting, bouncing, or conducting.^cn-behaviors-def
    
5. **Interactions**  
    How the Nature affects or is affected by other things. This includes interactions with matter, living organisms, chakra, other Natures, environments, and barriers.^cn-interactions-def
    
6. **Control Variables**  
    The aspects of the Nature the user can deliberately manipulate. Examples include quantity, shape, intensity, speed, density, range, temperature, and direction.^cn-ctrl-vars-def
    
7. **Environmental Dependencies**  
    Outside conditions that affect the Nature's performance. For example, Water might depend on available moisture, while Fire might behave differently in oxygen-rich versus oxygen-poor environments.^cn-env-dependencies-def
    
8. **Biological/Cognitive Requirements**  
    The physical and mental equipment required to produce and control the Nature. This includes chakra pathways, organs, neurological processing, visualization ability, concentration, and subconscious control.^cn-biological-and-cognitive-reqs-def
    
9. **Efficiency & Scaling**  
    **Efficiency** describes how much chakra is wasted while producing the desired effect; **scaling** describes how output changes when more chakra, skill, or physiological capacity is applied.^efficiency-scaling-def
    
10. **Failure Modes**  
    What happens when Nature transformation or control goes wrong. Examples include unstable manifestations, premature dissipation, loss of control, backlash, or accidental effects.^fail-modes-def
    
11. **Compatibility**  
    How readily the Nature works alongside other Natures or systems. It determines which combinations are naturally compatible, difficult to combine, or potentially incompatible.^compat-def
